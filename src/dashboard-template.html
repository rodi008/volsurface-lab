The implied volatility surface

Surface
Implied volatility across log-moneyness k = ln(K/F) and expiry, from an SVI fit per slice. Cells outside the strike range each expiry actually quotes are dimmed: there the surface is extrapolation, not price.
Smile detail
Exchange marks against the calibrated slice, with the fit residual below in vol points — the unit that can be compared against the bid-ask width.
02

Term structure

ATM vs model-free, log time axis
At-the-money implied volatility against the fair variance-swap strike. The gap between them is the convexity of the smile — the part of the price that ATM vol cannot see.
03

Wing structure and skew

25-delta risk reversal & butterfly
RR25 is the difference between the 25-delta call and put volatilities: what the market charges for asymmetry, largely independent of the level of vol. BF25 is the average wing over the ATM: what it charges for convexity.
04

Variance risk premium

By expiry
Fair variance-swap strike against realized volatility over a matched trailing window. The bar between them is the premium; the t-column is that premium in standard errors of the realized leg alone.
Ex post, through time
DVOL on each date against the variance actually realized over the following 30 days. The shaded band is the premium the variance seller was paid — filled toward the seller when positive, against them when negative.
05

Risk-neutral density

Breeden–Litzenberger
The second derivative of the call price with respect to strike is the risk-neutral density; the first derivative is the probability of finishing above a level. Both are exact identities, differentiated off the calibrated smile rather than off raw quotes.
q(K) = erT · ∂2C/∂K2     Q(ST > X) = −erT · ∂C/∂K |K=X
06

Calibration and arbitrage diagnostics

every claim above depends on these
A density read off a slice that violates the butterfly condition is meaningless, so the checks run before the results rather than after. Durrleman's g(k) ≥ 0 is the butterfly condition; total variance non-decreasing in maturity is the calendar condition; Roger Lee's moment formula caps wing slopes at 2.
